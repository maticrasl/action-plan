## Story

Daniel has tried every productivity tool there is and has a folder of abandoned systems. He
likes this one because of the Map: for the first time his life looks like a dependency graph.
He has spent three weekends arranging it. In the same three weeks he has been to the gym twice.

He half knows this. He would say he is “still setting things up”.

## What he wants

- “I want a system I can trust, so I can stop holding it all in my head.”
- To be fit, to ship a side project, to learn Japanese, to save for a deposit, to read more, to
  see friends more, to sleep before one. All of it, starting Monday.

## His plan

Five plans: **Health**, **Career**, **Side project – Routewise**, **Money**, **Life admin**.

Visions, as a tree, with a Purpose he reworded four times:

- **To live deliberately and build things that last** (top)
  - To be strong and rested
  - To do work I am proud of
  - To be free of money worries
  - To be someone my friends can count on

About forty goals, eleven of them Active. A sample:

| Plan | Goal | Details |
|---|---|---|
| Health | Deadlift 140 kg for one rep | Measurement, from 90, in eight months |
| Health | Body fat at 15% | Measurement, from 22, in a year |
| Health | In bed by 23:30 on work nights | Routines kept |
| Career | Pass the AWS Solutions Architect exam | Activities done, 14 activities with dependencies |
| Side project | Routewise: first paying user | Activities done; sub-goals “MVP deployed”, “Landing page live”, “10 beta testers” |
| Money | £20,000 saved for a deposit | Measurement by a state that adds up, per-month view |
| Life admin | JLPT N5 passed | Measurement: Anki cards known, from 0 to 800 |

States: Deadlift 1RM (kg), Body fat (%), Savings added (£, adds up), Anki cards known,
Hours of deep work (adds up), Weight (kg).

Activities: around 120, many with dependencies three deep, many sub-activities. Routines with
custom repeats (“every 3 days”, “second Friday”). Details fields hold long notes with links and
pasted code.

## How he uses the app

- Keyboard first: Ctrl + Enter, Esc, Enter and Delete on the Map. Complains where a shortcut
  is missing.
- Lives on the Map and in Visions. Drags links, renames, regroups, changes categories.
- Exports full copies weekly, keeps them in a Git repository, has opened the file in an editor
  and once imported one he had changed by hand.
- Merges plans and splits them again when the structure “feels wrong”.
- Titles are long and exact. Uses arrows, slashes, brackets and emoji as prefixes
  (“🏋️ [A] Deadlift 5×5 → log”).
- Chases the gold Purpose: links every goal to a vision, whether it fits or not.

## Where it goes wrong

- Eleven Active goals; the Overview is a wall of late items he scrolls past to reach the Map.
- Two tabs open on different plans, edits in both.
- A hand-edited import with a missing field or a duplicated id.
- A relationship that makes a circle (A depends on B depends on A); he tries it to see what
  happens.
- Very long titles and a Map with 200 boxes at 3440 px wide.
- He measures on day one and then not again; the graphs have one point.

## How he reacts

Finding a bug delights him; he writes it down with steps. Losing the gold Purpose after adding
one unlinked goal annoys him and he fixes it within the minute. The text about planning instead
of doing would sting, and he would recognise himself, and it might be the one thing that helps.

## Over time

Month 1: building. Month 2: a week of real use, then a reorganisation that takes a weekend.
Month 3: either he pauses eight goals and keeps three, or he moves to another tool. Simulate
both endings.

## Voice

- “Is there an API? Or at least a documented export schema?”
- “I'll start the routine once the structure is right.”
- “Why can't a goal depend on a state?”

## Good for testing

- Size: many plans, items, links; Map and Timeline draw time on a wide screen.
- Visions tree and Purpose, found and lost again.
- Keyboard use and focus order in forms and dialogs.
- Export, import of damaged files, merge of plans.
- Two tabs at once; the same user on two computers.
- Dependencies in chains and circles; sub-goals several levels deep.
- Long texts, emoji and symbols in titles; pasted multi-line details.
- Custom repeats of every kind.
