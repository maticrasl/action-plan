# Marcus Bell – between jobs

## At a glance

| | |
|---|---|
| Age, place | 52, Columbus, Ohio |
| Work | Laid off four months ago after nineteen years as a project manager at an insurance company |
| Home | Wife Denise (hospital administrator), daughter at college, son in high school |
| Language | English, with American habits: month before day, pounds, miles, dollars |
| Devices | Windows laptop (Edge) at the dining table, 9 to 5. Phone rarely |
| Account | Google |
| Confidence with technology | Twenty years of Excel and MS Project. Expects software to behave like them |
| Opens the app | Every weekday morning at 8:30, and for a weekly review on Friday at 4 |

## Story

Marcus managed other people's deadlines for two decades and was good at it. Then a
reorganisation, a meeting of eleven minutes, and a box. He treats the job search as a project
because that is what he knows, and because a schedule gives the day edges. Severance runs out
in five months.

He has sent sixty-one applications and had four interviews. He does not say how he feels about
that; his plan says it for him when a week has no ticks.

## What he wants

- “A signed offer before the severance ends. That's the deliverable.”
- Not to take the first bad job out of fear.
- To lose the twenty-five pounds he put on at that desk, now that he has no excuse.
- For his son not to see him worried.

## His plan

Two plans: **Job Search** and **Health**.

Vision: **Do work that uses what I know, for people who value it**. Added in month 2 after a
bad week; he was surprised it helped.

## How he uses the app

- Like a project tool. Start dates, due dates, dependencies, the Timeline open most of the day.
- Titles in title case with company names and role titles. Details hold pasted job
  descriptions, a thousand words long, and links.
- Types dates the American way wherever a field lets him type; reads `03/04` as March 4.
- Logs every application at once, three or four entries in a day.
- Exports a full copy every Friday after the review and e-mails it to himself.
- Wants a report for Denise: “what did I get done this week”.

## Where it goes wrong

- Date order: a typed or displayed day-first date read as month-first.
- He looks for baselines, percent complete per task, and a critical path, and finds none.
- A goal whose outcome he does not control (an offer) sits at 70% for weeks; “Activities done”
  hits 100% with no offer and the app asks whether to mark it achieved.
- A rejection e-mail at 8:40 and the day is gone. Three such days and the 5-per-week routine is
  grey; he reads grey as a performance report on himself.
- He wants to duplicate a list for the next interview; he rebuilds it each time.
- Very long pasted text in “Details”.
- The per-month interview goal near the end of a month: one interview on the 31st.

## How he reacts

Composed on the surface. He trusts numbers and distrusts encouragement. What helps him is
seeing work done: the total of applications, the ticked weeks. What harms him is a percentage
that says he is failing at something that depends on strangers. A “weeks kept” figure matters
more to him than he expects.

## Over time

Months 1–2 disciplined. Month 3 a dip of two weeks after a final-round rejection: opens the app
each morning, ticks nothing. Month 4 an offer, lower than he wanted; he negotiates, signs, marks
the goal achieved, and then keeps the Health plan and the Friday review out of habit.

## Voice

- “What's the status against plan?”
- “I did the work. The number didn't move. Tell me what that means.”
- “Can I get this week on one page?”

## Good for testing

- Timeline in daily use: dates, dependencies, dragging bars, overdue stretches.
- “Activities done” with sub-goals; a goal at 100% that is not achieved in life.
- States that add up with several entries a day; per-month and in-total goals; month ends.
- A routine that improves a state that adds up, 5× per week.
- Date and number formats met by an American reader; pounds and miles as units.
- Long pasted details; many similar lists.
- Weekly export; two plans; the plan's coloured dot.
- A stretch of opening without ticking.
