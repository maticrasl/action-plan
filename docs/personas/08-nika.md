## Story

Nika missed the national final in the 100 m freestyle by four tenths. Her coach said: write
down what you swim and what you sleep, then we talk. Her mother already used the app for the
household, Luka for his exams, so Nika got “her own corner” on the family computer.

She is disciplined in the water and chaotic out of it. School is slipping: maths is at 2. And
there is something she is working on that her mother must not see.

## What she wants

- »da grem pod minuto. to je to.«
- State championship final in June.
- Maths up to 4 so her mother stops talking about quitting swimming.
- To eat enough. She has written it down as a goal and it is the private one.

## How she uses the app

- On the phone in the changing room, sideways, with wet fingers: tick the training, type the
  kilometres into the “how much?” popup, done in ten seconds.
- On Sunday on the PC: sleep and times from the paper log, the graphs for the coach, a
  screenshot sent to him.
- Lower case, no full stops, emoji in titles and plan names, abbreviations (dp, mat, tr).
- Long-presses everything. Found the context menus on day one.
- Checks who is logged in on the PC before she starts, and logs out after. Mostly.

## Where it goes wrong

- A time like `1:02,4` does not fit a number field; she converts to seconds, and past a minute
  she sometimes types `1.02`.
- Lower is better: does the goal show progress when the number falls, and what happens to the
  graph's target line?
- Training camp and taper weeks: 45 km one week, 12 the next. The per-week goal swings.
- Illness: a week with every set-day training grey.
- The family PC: once she added three trainings while Luka was the one logged in. She wants to
  know how safe her plan is from her mother. It is kept apart; it is not locked.
- Her mother's account syncs on the same machine; Nika's phone and the PC edit the same routine
  on the same evening.
- Emoji in a plan name in the sidebar, in the plan switcher on the phone, in an export's file
  name.
- The “eat normally” routine: a day she does not tick is a day she does not want to look at.

## How she reacts

Competitive with her own numbers; a best time entered is the high point of the week. Grey days
from illness irritate her (“nisem jaz kriva”). She would stop using the app at once if she
thought her mother could read it. The eating goal is the one where the app's tone matters; the
text about tracking food and talking to someone is written for a person like her, and she would
pretend not to have read it.

## Over time

Season rhythm: build, camp, taper, race, a week off. Summer break from school empties the
second plan. After the June championship (she makes the final, swims 1:00,3) she changes the
goal's date and carries on.

## Good for testing

- Several users on one device: switching, a tab left on another user, privacy texts.
- Phone landscape (icon rail), fast one-hand ticking, the amount popup.
- States that add up with several entries a day, counted per week; uneven weeks.
- A measured goal whose target is below its start.
- Set-day routines six days a week; make-up; routine list daily.
- Emoji and lower case everywhere; Slovenian slang without care for spelling.
- Same account on phone and PC edited the same evening.
- Graphs shown to someone else; a screenshot as the “report”.
- Sensitive goal about eating, kept by a minor.
