# Sam Whitaker – one day at a time

## At a glance

| | |
|---|---|
| Age, place | 36, Bristol |
| Work | Sous-chef in a busy restaurant; shifts from 2 p.m. to past midnight, Tuesday to Saturday |
| Home | Lives with a flatmate; a seven-year-old daughter, Ivy, every second weekend |
| Language | English |
| Devices | One Android phone (Firefox). No computer |
| Account | Guest. Will not log in with Google |
| Confidence with technology | Fine, and suspicious. Reads the privacy text before anything else |
| Opens the app | Once a day, around 1 a.m., on the night bus or in bed |

## Story

Kitchens run on adrenaline, and after service on drink. Sam's drinking stopped being a staff
habit some years ago. Ten weeks back they missed Ivy's school play. They have not had a drink
for thirty-four days, are down from twenty cigarettes to eight, and go to a meeting on Monday
nights.

Sam chose this app because it says the plans stay on the phone. Nobody at work knows. The
flatmate does not know. An app that asked for an e-mail address would have been closed.

## What they want

- “To be someone Ivy can rely on. That's all of it.”
- Not to drink today.
- To stop smoking by her birthday.
- To have five hundred pounds put by, for the first time in their adult life.

## How they use the app

- After the shift, after midnight. The day that just ended is, by the calendar, yesterday. Sam
  ticks “Dry day” for it through the week strip, every single night.
- Short, flat titles that give nothing away. No details. No category.
- Keeps the phone on its darkest setting; uses the app with one hand, tired.
- Looks at the streak tag on “Dry day” more than at anything else.
- Exports a full copy every couple of weeks to the phone's downloads since the scare (below),
  and does not know what to do with the file.

## Where it goes wrong

- The day boundary. At 00:50 the app's “today” is a day with nothing done yet, and the day they
  are proud of is one step back. On a tired night they tick the wrong one and the streak looks
  broken.
- Five weeks in, they cleared the browser's data to free space. Everything went. They rebuilt
  the plan from memory and had to enter day 23 as day 1; getting the earlier ticks back in
  meant going back week by week.
- “Cigs today” should go down. The goal runs from 20 to 0; a day at 11 after a day at 7 shows as
  going backwards, on the worst day to be told that.
- A routine of not doing something: when is a dry day “done”? Only when it is over.
- They opened the app in a private tab once, by habit, and saw an empty app.
- If there were a slip, what then? One missed day on a daily routine. Sam has thought about
  what the app would show.
- No reminders, and for once that suits the person.

## How they react

Everything in this plan is loaded. A broken streak caused by the app, not by them, would be
close to unforgivable. A missed day shown in grey and not red is the difference between
carrying on and saying “sod it”. Sam does not want praise, confetti or exclamation marks; a
quiet number that goes up is enough. Any hint that the data leaves the phone ends it.

## Over time

Day 34 now. Simulate both roads: ninety days straight, the goal achieved, a new one of 180; and
a slip on day 51 after a double shift, two days unticked, then the return and one tick. The
smoking line falls in steps with bad weeks in it. “Tell Mum” gets done somewhere around day 70.

## Voice

- “Where does this go? Who can see it?”
- “It's ten to one. It's still Tuesday as far as I'm concerned.”
- “I don't need a gold star. I need it to not lose my days.”

## Good for testing

- Use after midnight: ticking yesterday, streaks across the day boundary.
- Guest only: site data cleared, private tabs, export as the only backup, import to restore.
- Privacy and data texts, read first and read closely.
- Daily routine with a long streak; one miss; the look of a missed day; back on track with one
  tick.
- A measured goal that falls to zero, with noisy daily values.
- Custom repeat every two weeks; routine list on work days only.
- An activity with no dates that stays for months.
- Tone of every message, for someone in recovery.
- Dark surroundings, small phone, one hand.
