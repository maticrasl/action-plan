## Story

Priya was diagnosed with ADHD at nineteen. She is bright, behind on two modules, and has a
drawer of planners used up to page six. She found the app at 1 a.m. in exam season, loaded a
study template, added nine goals before two, and felt wonderful. She opened it again four days
later, saw a column of red, and closed it.

She keeps coming back, because the deadlines are real: exams have dates that do not move.

## What she wants

- “I just want to not be in a panic the week before every deadline.”
- To pass Pharmacology, which she failed in January and resits in eleven weeks.
- To stop living on toast. To answer her mum's messages. To get her deposit back in June.

## Her plan

Two plans: **Uni** (from a template) and **sort my life out**.

No visions. She saw the view and thought “later”.

## How she uses the app

- Fast. Taps before reading. Dismissed the tour at step two.
- Adds things in bursts: fifteen items in ten minutes, titles in lower case, some unfinished
  (“email dr h about”).
- Fills the goal form with the least that lets it save: “idk”, “because”, “yes”.
- After a break she deletes half the plan or starts a new plan from the template again; there
  are three copies of “Uni” by month 2.
- Uses the library computers between lectures, logs in, and walks away without logging out.
- Up after midnight most nights; ticks yesterday's things at 00:40.

## Where it goes wrong

- Too much is Active. Every return starts with a wall of overdue items.
- Fixed deadlines and no time left: she needs to cut scope, and instead adds more steps.
- Duplicate plans from starting the template again; she no longer knows which one is current.
- Two Google accounts on one phone: her plans “disappeared” when she logged in with the
  other one.
- Her account left on a public computer, next to someone else's.
- The pomodoro routine at weekdays, four a day, is too big; it has been grey for weeks.
- She changes a deadline by dragging on the Timeline with a thumb and moves the wrong bar.

## How she reacts

The first session of a burst is joy. A red list after a break is shame, and shame makes her
close the app, which makes the break longer. One line that says “pick one thing for today”
would work on her; a list of what she missed would not. When something she ticked moves a
progress bar, she will do two more things just to see it move.

## Over time

A saw-tooth: burst, drop, three weeks of nothing, panic return nine days before a deadline,
three intense days, a pass (just), silence. The “sort my life out” plan decays; the Uni plan
is rebuilt each term.

## Voice

- “ok new plan. for real this time”
- “why is everything red I literally just made this”
- “can I just hide all of that and see today”

## Good for testing

- Templates, used repeatedly; duplicate plans; deleting plans.
- Returning after weeks: overdue load on the Overview, late routines back on track with one
  tick, moving dates on the Timeline by touch.
- Many Active goals; pausing in bulk (she would want it).
- Goal form filled with nonsense; untouched goals.
- Two Google accounts for one person; several users on a public computer; “Remove from this
  device”.
- Use across midnight.
- A state that adds up counted in total; lists half done.
- Slow laptop.
