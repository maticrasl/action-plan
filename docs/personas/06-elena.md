# Elena Marchetti – starting over

## At a glance

| Work | Freelance translator (Italian, English, German), works from home |

## Story

Elena moved for a marriage that ended. She stayed because going back felt like losing twice.
She works alone, knows four people in the city, and understands half of what the neighbours
say. Her therapist in Bologna, whom she still sees online, suggested writing down what a good
life here would look like.

What she wants has no numbers. The goal form asked her for a metric and a target and she sat
in front of it for ten minutes.

## What she wants

- “I want to feel that I live here, not that I am waiting here.”
- To speak Slovenian well enough to joke.
- To be calmer. To stop checking her ex-husband's profile.
- To have people to eat with.

## Her plan

One plan: **A life in Ljubljana**.

Visions, written with care, as if already true:

- **I feel at home where I live** (top)
  - I have people here who would notice if I disappeared
  - I am at peace with how my marriage ended
  - I speak the language of the place I live in

## How she uses the app

- Slowly and thoughtfully. Reads help texts and guide texts in full; they are what she likes
  best.
- Writes long descriptions and details, in careful English with the odd Italian word and
  accented letters (è, à, perché). Slovenian words with č, š, ž, sometimes misspelt.
- Rewrites her visions often. Opens the Visions view just to read them.
- Rates the week every Sunday and writes why in the state's description, because there is
  nowhere else for it.
- Switches the interface to Slovenian for a day “as homework”, gets lost, switches back.

## Where it goes wrong

- The goal form: no number fits “feel at home”. She did not find “Routines kept” by herself and
  nearly gave up on day one.
- “Why is this realistic?” made her doubt the whole goal.
- A self-rated state gives a jagged line; a bad week reads as failure.
- In a low week every routine is missed and the Overview fills with grey and red; she does not
  open the app, and feels worse for not opening it.
- “Non guardare il suo profilo” is a routine of *not* doing something: when does she tick it?
  At the end of the day, and after midnight it is the next day.
- After switching language her own item names stay in English and Italian; mixed screens.
- She achieved “Know my neighbourhood” and felt nothing, and did not know what should come
  next.

## How she reacts

She notices tone more than function. A text that sounds like a manager makes her feel judged; a
text that sounds like a decent person keeps her. Red marks hit her hard. She will pause a goal
if the app tells her it is allowed. She would never want anything shared or public.

## Over time

Four steady weeks; a bad fortnight around the anniversary of the divorce, with nothing ticked
and one calm rating of 2; a careful return in which she pauses two goals. By month 4 the
language exam is the engine of the plan and the calm line has crept from 4 to 6.

## Voice

- “I cannot put a number on this. That is exactly the problem.”
- “Is it bad that I changed my vision again?”
- “Today I spoke with the lady at the market for five minutes. I want to write this somewhere.”

## Good for testing

- Goals without numbers: “Routines kept”, “Activities done”, self-rated states.
- Visions tree, edited often; a goal reached and what follows.
- Texts: tone of help, empty states, late marks; read by a careful non-native reader.
- Language switch back and forth; mixed-language content; accents and diacritics.
- Set days with make-up; routines of abstaining; states that add up counted per week.
- A stretch of two bad weeks and the return.
- Phone and laptop in step; long descriptions on a small screen.
