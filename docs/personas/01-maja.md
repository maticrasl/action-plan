# Maja Kovač – the shift nurse

## At a glance

| | |
|---|---|
| Age, place | 34, Ljubljana |
| Work | Nurse on an internal medicine ward; mornings, afternoons and nights in no fixed order |
| Home | Partner Andrej, children Tine (5) and Zala (2) |
| Language | Slovenian interface, Slovenian item names |
| Devices | Android phone (Chrome), app installed on the home screen. Nothing else |
| Account | Guest for the first three weeks, then logs in with Google after a scare |
| Confidence with technology | Ordinary. Uses what works, does not explore |
| Opens the app | Four to six times a week, for under a minute, often one-handed |

## Story

Maja has been tired for two years. The roster comes out a month ahead and changes anyway. She
used to run; the last time was before Zala. A colleague showed her the app in the break room.
What caught her was that a routine could ask for “twice a week” and not “Monday and Thursday”,
because she has no Mondays and Thursdays.

She does not want a new life. She wants to sleep, to run again, and to have one evening a week
that is not work, children or laundry.

## What she wants

- »Rada bi imela energijo. Za otroka in še malo zase.«
- To run five kilometres again without stopping.
- To stop scrolling in bed after a night shift.

## How she uses the app

- On the tram, in the changing room, in bed. Opens the Overview, ticks, closes. Never the Map,
  rarely the Timeline.
- Ticks a routine a day or two late, through the arrows on the week strip.
- Titles are short and lower-effort; details are empty. Uses the decimal comma.
- Measures the run only after a good run. Bad runs are not recorded.
- The phone is often at 10% battery and on hospital Wi-Fi that drops.

## Where it goes wrong

- After a night shift she ticks “today” at 6:40 in the morning for the evening before, or the
  other way round. Days and shifts do not line up for her.
- The set-day routine (phone out of the bedroom) shows missed days in weeks with night shifts,
  when she is not in the bedroom at all. She does not know what to do with those days.
- She paused a goal and then could not find it, and thought it was deleted.
- In week 3 her phone's storage cleaner ran and she believed the plan was gone. It was not, but
  that is when she logged in with Google. She does not understand “sync is paused” and ignores
  the cloud button until something looks wrong.
- “Kupi tekaške copate” has been red for weeks. She will not move the date; she does not know
  she can.

## How she reacts

Red marks do not hurt her much; she sees red all day at work. What makes her close the app is
length: a form with many fields on a day with no time. A streak tag pleases her more than she
would admit. If the app ever lost her ticks, she would not come back.

## Over time

Weeks 1–2 daily and keen. Weeks 3–5 steady, two runs most weeks. Week 6 both children are ill
and she works extra shifts: nothing for nine days. Week 8 she comes back, ticks today, and
carries on without tidying anything.

## Voice

- »Nimam časa brati. Samo povej, kaj naj kliknem.«
- »Dvakrat na teden, kdaj, bom pa sama videla.«
- »Aha, a to se da prestavit? Tega nisem vedla.«

## Good for testing

- Routines by number of times per week; make-up days; ticking earlier weeks.
- Routine list on a phone, ticked with a thumb.
- Guest data taken along at the first Google login; “sync is paused” met by someone who does
  not read it.
- Weak connection, changes made offline.
- Coming back after nine days: what the Overview shows first.
- Use just after midnight and early in the morning.
- Paused goals: can they be found again.
